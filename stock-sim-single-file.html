Walkthrough: How the Simulator Works

1) Start State

You begin with $100,000 in cash, a live watchlist seeded with recent real-world market levels, and a simulated calendar that progresses only when the market is open. Your game auto-saves locally every few seconds and after every trade.

2) Price Formation

Every tick combines a market factor, sector factor, stock-specific volatility, mean reversion to a valuation anchor, and active news/event impacts. The result drives intraday candles, volume, spreads, and option pricing.

  • Market factor: broad sentiment plus macro variables such as VIX, oil, and rates.
  • Sector factor: technology, financials, energy, consumer, and index-level moves.
  • Idiosyncratic factor: company-specific drift, volatility, and catalyst risk.

3) Trading Stocks

Use the stock ticket to submit market, limit, or stop orders. You can go long, sell existing shares, short stock, or cover a short. Orders placed while the market is closed remain queued until the next session.

4) Options Trading

The options chain is generated from the current underlying price, time to expiry, implied volatility, and the risk-free rate using a Black-Scholes style model. You can trade long or short calls and puts. Short premium positions reserve margin; covered calls use eligible long shares as collateral.

5) News, Events, Earnings, and Dividends

The feed generates company-specific stories, macro releases, OPEC-style energy shocks, guidance changes, analyst notes, earnings surprises, dividends, and rare splits. Events can raise volatility, change trend, and push prices over multiple sessions.

6) Indicators and Risk

The chart displays candles with optional SMA 20, SMA 50, and Bollinger Bands. Below it, the simulator computes RSI and MACD. The portfolio view tracks equity, margin used, available buying power, realized P/L, unrealized P/L, and approximate portfolio Greeks.

7) Saving

Your portfolio, market state, open orders, news feed, and price history are saved automatically to localStorage. Use Save for a manual snapshot or Reset to clear the save and start a fresh simulation.

8) Tips

  • Watch the news feed before taking leverage.
  • Short options create income but also add margin risk and assignment risk.
  • High RSI and stretched Bollinger bands can mark overbought conditions, but momentum can persist.
  • Use expiries and strikes that match your thesis: momentum, hedge, income, or speculation.
